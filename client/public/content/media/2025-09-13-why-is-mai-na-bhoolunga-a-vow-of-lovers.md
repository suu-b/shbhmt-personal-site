In 1974, [Roti, Kapdaa Makaan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roti_Kapada_Aur_Makaan) (Food, Cloth, and Shelter) was released — an Indian film starring [Manoj Kumar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manoj_Kumar), [Jeenat Aman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeenat_Aman), and [Moushumi Chatterjee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moushumi_Chatterjee) in the lead roles. The film’s title refers to the necessities of life and likely borrowed the phrase from the slogan with the same phrase from the Indian PM, [Indira Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indira_Gandhi).  
In the film Manoj, following his usual archetype, struggles against poverty while keeping a patriotic heart. Throughout, however, except for the three necessities from the film’s name, we come across yet another necessity for survival, at least among societies — love.

---

The song [Mai Na Bhoolunga](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UGl34-cDPa8) is a vow made by lovers. This vow is usually momentary — it lasts for just a tiny second when the lovers stare into each other’s eyes — even for a second. And in that fraction, make vows of forever understanding, warmth, and oneness.
I love to divide the song as per its existing structure conceptually. And I would be really glad if you would plug your headphones, and with me, embark through these 6 minutes into the eyes of two lovers, who, among blooms of new spring, are vowing togetherness.

### To forget whatever impelled us and thus, merge into oneness

```
चलो जग को भूलें, खयालों में झूलें
बहारों में डोलें, सितारों को छूलें
आ तेरी मैं माँग संवारूँ तू दुल्हन बन जाये
माँग से जो दुल्हन का रिश्ता मैं ना भूलूँगी…
```

It’s the urge to leave the world aside that impelled all of us and drowned us in the depths of the deep lake of loneliness. And thus, forgetting the world, dance among flowers and rise above and touch the starry sky. And for the oneness vow that one may fashion vermillion on his lover’s hair, thus becoming her by marrying her, and so the bride and the vermillion (her groom), they shall never leave one another.

### A glance at the coming times of uncertainty

```
समय की धारा में, उमर बह जानी है
जो घड़ी जी लेंगे, वही रह जानी है
मैं बन जाऊँ साँस आखिरी तू जीवन बन जाये
जीवन से साँसों का रिश्ता मैं ना भूलूँगी…
बरसता सावन हो, महकता आँगन हो
कभी दिल दूल्हा हो, कभी दिल दुल्हन हो
गगन बनकर झूमें, पवन बनकर झूमें
चलो हम राह मोड़ें, कभी ना संग छोड़ें
तरस चख जाना है, नज़र चख जाना है
```

They wonder that their life shall pass — pass in a moment, and thus, they must live but every moment of their life. The lover vows to become the breath of her husband, and thus, could never be separated from her, as both breath and life are inseparable.
Life is uncertain — they realize (as portrayed through the imagery of rain). If the rain shall come to stop them, or wind, the lovers vow to change the path and not each other.
<br/>

### To the unconditioned oneness

```
कहीं पे बस जाएंगे, ये दिन कट जाएंगे
अरे क्या बात चली, वो देखो रात ढली
ये बातें चलती रहें, ये रातें ढलती रहें
```

Eventually, they shall settle, shall pass the life, but in each other’s warmth.
And with this, the lovers realize that the night has come, and their talk will last for ages.

### A Prayer

```
मैं मन को मंदिर कर डालूँ तू पूजन बन जाये
मंदिर से पूजा का रिश्ता मैं ना…
```

As the song ends, the lover decides to let his mind be a temple. And so, his lover is the Prayer. Temple and Prayer cannot be separated. Thus, they shall live, live, and love for years to come.
And a line echoes throughout the song — ‘Mai na bhoolunga’, that the vows they made in this moment, they shall never forget.

---

This is the beauty of ‘Ma na bhoolunga’, it is a song expanding the momentary meeting of the eyes of two lovers. And if felt by heart, the warmest feeling. May God bless us with such love.
