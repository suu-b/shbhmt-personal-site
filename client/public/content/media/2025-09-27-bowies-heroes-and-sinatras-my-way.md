Not much time has passed since I started exploring English Music. Still in these few months, I have discovered many of the gems from modern times all the way to the 60s. At the same time, I've come to realize the power of music in terms of expression. If I have to arrange all the ways humans can express things (i.e., all the forms of art), I would have placed Music at the top of the tier list. The reason is that this curated arrangement of harmonies really bears this immense power to morph our feelings.  
In all of the English music, Rock has particularly grown on me. Though I am not a person who is extremely energetic or literally passionate in a human form. Still, Rock music feels to me as if it is trying to elevate me to a certain realm of ecstasy. It appeals to my deepest feelings, whims, insecurities, and whatnot. It fills me with passion to move.

### Drawing a line between them  
In [John Armstrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Armstrong_(British_writer))'s [Love, Life, Goethe](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/101478.Love_Life_Goethe), I read this:   
<br/>
*… he says – that such a view of life is entirely wrong. It’s just that it’s so unhelpful. The trouble with terms such as ‘fate’ is that as events unfold there are no rules that allow us to be sure where the hand of fate is pointing, we can perfectly well understand what is meant by fate – it’s a pattern we discern retrospectively - but we don’t have any sure grounds for assigning this or that event in the present to the workings of fate. And it seems to be just this point that is bothering the stranger. What he objects is that a young man should try to apply this term it might have some use old age – when we look back over the connections and see (once we know how things did turn out) how this or that event provided the right step stone (unrecognized as such at that time) to a later destination. But to believe in fate before the outcome is known is dangerous. It undermines our sense of freedom and responsibility.* 
<br/>
It advises young men not to have a teleological optimism towards life. It is this feeling that whatever happened, happened for a reason, and it is good. Honestly, this is my outlook towards life generally, and thus, this line kinda bugged me. At the same time, however, I understand what the advice intends to say. It warns against such optimism as it may let us submit to our inner fears/assumptions. We will simply go away saying - 'that is how it was meant to be: for the best'. Well, surely it may be true - this optimistic feeling, however, I agree that this philosophy has often let me lose chances. They may not be just professional chances but interpersonal as well. We humans have this ill-fated condition to keep 'choosing' at all instances of our living. We always want to make the best choices. Still, we let go of many options, submitting to our fears. And this is, I agree, dangerous. It will often render our potential dumb. We will be condemned to lead mediocre lives.  


Then, there is this feeling of intense passion, freedom, and fearlessness. This feeling is truly what fuels social, intellectual, and even personal revolutions. And one must surely live through every condition to break one's inherent suppositions. Then, one can carefully cherry-pick what works and what does not. However, this surely renders one vulnerable many times, but we cannot cross the sea by just staring at it. 

But I do not wholly agree with the above two arguments.

One has ups. One has downs. My Way' is an expression of sympathy to oneself. It just translates to - 'I did my best'. It is the teleological optimism backwards that whatever happened to me happened for the best. It surely heals the listener. It motivates him to rise again. To not feel bad about the past. This seems extremely important. It heals the mental wounds of a person.  
Then, 'Heroes' attempts to fill the bare chest with passion again. It just says - 'You are unstoppable' and 'Rise, Rise from your ashes'. It calls for an uprising. It is surely passion.  

These two songs are opposites, but at the same time, essential for a being.
