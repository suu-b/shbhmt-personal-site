_And Almitra the seeress said, Blessed be this day and this place and your spirit that has spoken.  
And he answered, Was it I who spoke?  
Was I not also a listener?_

<br/>

This excerpt from [Gibran’s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kahlil_Gibran) [The Prophet](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/2547.The_Prophet) pretty much summarizes all the ranting I am going to do here.  
Nevertheless, I often spend some proportion of my time writing poems. Lately, I’ve been sending them off to an array of journals. Consequently, I’ve been receiving an array of rejections too. Today, this whole sequence pushed me into a trance. Though it was not the infamous imposter syndrome, I realize that I genuinely find poetry worth pursuing. Expressing my deepest feelings in words is a fascinating idea. It is art.  
I decided to turn inwards and ask — ‘Is my poetry truly art?’  
What art is? I asked.<br/>

---

I really like definitions. The initial definition of art that I thought of was:
<br/>

_Art is a collective name for a twofold phenomenon — expression and resonance._
<br/>

One expresses their deepest feelings through colors, music, or words; this is expression; the expression evokes thought beyond mere function among its beholders; this is resonance. Well, then, a third phenomenon enters: validation. People who do art strive for validation (including me). Sometimes, unhealthily. They even make validation the principle of all other phenomena in their artistic endeavors — that the existence of their art is based on ‘validation’. It isn’t.  
I slam this third ‘phenomenon’. This is because it is not original. It is a further construct built on top of resonance. Validation is just resonance scaled and politicized for various reasons, not a foundation of art. Art is created, never bestowed. I can even claim that resonance also does not need to be evoked in anyone else but the creator of the art himself.  
This is a libertarian stance — it slices away external elements and adds to our definition of art:
<br/>
_Art is a private dialogue between the artist and the artist’s own self. It is a twofold phenomenon consisting of expression and resonance.  
It begins with the artist gazing into the eyes of his own self, dissolving into it, and wrestling with all that has happened, all that he has dreamt, and all that he whims.  
Expression lets the artist materialize tangible elements out of that wrestling. Resonance is when a meaning beyond mere function comes to reside in those elements._
<br/>
But then someone can ask: ‘If expression solely creates art, and resonance can exist solely in the creator, then the very idea of the audience is irrelevant?’. Yes, if art is solely what we are talking about — art is a private dialogue, a process.  
The result of this private dialogue is what I define as ‘artwork’. It is public. It is the relic of the process when the artist dissolved into the intangible. It is just a residue. The paintings, the concertos, the poems — they are all residues of that sacred process called ‘art’, yet we enjoy them to the fullest when we experience them. Imagine how awesome it would be to be the very witness of that process.? To see the artist wrestling with the intangible, remembering the past, pondering on it, bringing it to some tangible medium — this whole trance, it will be relishing to witness.  
If people resonate with the artwork — the residue of art — then let it be famous. Let it be validated or disvalidated for a myriad of factors and reasons.

A true artist will feel a distance between himself and the artwork. They are not its creator; rather, they are the midwife. The artwork was just created during the art. They didn’t wholly make it. Therefore, for a true artist, the art will be the primary enjoyment, not the artwork (here comes the classical enjoy the journey, not the destination quote).  
<br/>

_And a beholder who just resonated with his art would say, ‘Blessed be this day and this place and your spirit that had created this masterpiece.  
And the artist shall answer — ‘Was it I who painted?  
‘Was I not just a participant?_

---

Well, then, this discussion lists two learnings for me:
I should not let rejections cast me down.
I should reflect more on the process and less on the product.
