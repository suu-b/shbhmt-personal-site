Months ago, I published an article - _On Artist and the Art_; it was largely my take on how metaphysically elevated experience art is, and how the artistic piece, which remains as the sole witness of that process except the artist, is merely a residue. Still, a beholder who commits oneself to feel the piece but with all the senses and the fullest heart, partially but lives that elevated experience that the artist went through. 
I find myself illiterate when it comes to the history of visual art. I do draw and paint occasionally, however. For the last few months, a certain style of art has grown over me, though I find myself totally incapable of attempting it. This is the Impressionist school.
 
***
### Introduction to Impressionism
Impressionism emerged as a countermovement against the overly conservative views of the standards of French Art Academia. Essentially, they were a bunch of artists with a taste for painting outdoors with a non-conservative style. When the French Academia rejected their art, they hosted independent exhibitions, which brought them prominence. Their beginning was really interesting, and I encourage you to read it. One helpful link - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impressionism#Beginnings).


Human Experience - perception - is an important element of Impressionism. Their main concern wasn't precision or realism but perception - how it appears to the eye - how pleasing - how refreshing. Monet is my reference for this discussion primarily. Look at his [Impression, Sunrise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impression,_Sunrise); this painting earned the name **Impressionism**. How wonderful the painting is. The sky and the water converge with it, smooches each other, and elegantly fade into each other. One, with a close eye, can see the industrial Paris hidden in the blue. And the sun, and its reflection, were painted with discrete yellow strokes of the brush in the middle. 
Another one of Monet - [Woman with a Parasol – Madame Monet and Her Son](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/1b/Claude_Monet_-_Woman_with_a_Parasol_-_Madame_Monet_and_Her_Son_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg), it features his wife and son. Can you feel how refreshing this painting is? See the grass, the flowers perched on the green, and the clouds over their head.  
And then there is this painting [Cliff Walk at Pourville](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a4/Claude_Monet_-_Cliff_Walk_at_Pourville_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg/1280px-Claude_Monet_-_Cliff_Walk_at_Pourville_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg), it features two figures, probably women, standing at the bay. Somehow instantly, while I looked at this piece, a novel's title came floating to me - [By river Piedra I sat down and wept](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/1428.By_the_River_Piedra_I_Sat_Down_and_Wept). At that point, I realized how deeply personal these pieces can get. They are so close to nature, yet somehow grew close to my inner life as well.

Another artist who attracted my eye is Pierre-Auguste Renoir. And what appeals to me the most in his work is the way he paints humans, especially women. How pure their appearance is - how feminine - gentle, blissful, a kinda motherly feeling runs all over them. Again, they somehow become deeply personal. See, for instance, [Bal du moulin de la Galette](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/40/Auguste_Renoir_-_Dance_at_Le_Moulin_de_la_Galette_-_Mus%C3%A9e_d%27Orsay_RF_2739_%28derivative_work_-_AutoContrast_edit_in_LCH_space%29.jpg/960px-Auguste_Renoir_-_Dance_at_Le_Moulin_de_la_Galette_-_Mus%C3%A9e_d%27Orsay_RF_2739_%28derivative_work_-_AutoContrast_edit_in_LCH_space%29.jpg) and [Luncheon of the Boating Party](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8d/Pierre-Auguste_Renoir_-_Luncheon_of_the_Boating_Party_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg/1280px-Pierre-Auguste_Renoir_-_Luncheon_of_the_Boating_Party_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg).

### Project
Well, I believe I have etched an impression on you, probably how intriguing and lovely Impressionist art has grown for me. Honestly, I am flying at this point, to such an extent that I want to do a project myself. It will be beneficial to me in two ways - I would learn, obviously, many aspects of art. Moreover, it will allow me to closely read many other Impressionists and their art style. 
I am thinking of working on a long project to paint an Impressionist-style art piece myself. It is a tough endeavor, given my tough hand at things like proportion and coloring. But I must not feel belittled at this yet. I mean, the core philosophy of Impressionism is never perfection in the conventional sense. Such perfection remains unachievable. But the perfection in expression - to express how I see what I see. That is the core thing to do. And yes, practicing proportions will only help in better expressing myself. I want to feel every corner of the space I look at. I want to ride the wings of imagination and surf but gently on the air that runs through lakes, through leaves, and shakes trees to let their flowers a seat of calm Earth. I want to feel, and for that I surely must vow two things: a commitment to discipline and to show up no matter how unmotivated I feel. Self-learning can grow really tough, but it will help me further my boundaries.

Let us see how it goes - the art and what exquisite experience they let themselves through - the artpiece and the artist.
