Recently, I have been solving DS&A problems again (woohoo!). But it is not due to that familiar surge of grinding that hits us early-career software professionals occasionally. Or maybe it is. But I assure you it is something deeper. Again, not a result of those 3 am yt videos titled - Lock the f* in - scaring the shit out of us. It is none of that. <br/>
When I began my degree in 2023, I soon grew to believe that this field isn't for me; that I have been pushed into this as a result of my good grades; that I didn't make a conscious decision at all. I am not going to blame my family or teachers, but myself - that I never actually cared where I was going. The whole idea in my mind was - I've got good grades, CS got money and scope - here's the future. And this single argument without any genuine inquiry led me here. <br/>
I am an optimistic person, and there's a reason - life makes sense if seen backwards (as Kierkegaard roughly says). Somehow pieces really do fall into place. <br/>
Sometimes I look back - initially, I had an interest in history; it matured into literature and philosophy, then poetry grew over me. I started doing poetry. I started watching a lot of cinema. I began reading different books. Literature, Philosophy, films, poetry, books held my shoulders and raised me up. When I was doing an internship, one bad day I got disillusioned by every fake castle that I had constructed in my mind. I looked back, then around, then forward, and trust me - I questioned - What the hell am I doing? <br/>
I wouldn't act pretentious. Things are still not totally figured out. But that is the theme of being in young adulthood. <br/>
Due to the disillusionment, I looked around - into CS - and somehow saw beauty hiding beneath code. I know I am being dramatic. But this is one of those peculiar traits of mine. Initially, the cutting-edge technologies - AI, quantum computing - they all appealed to me. I imagined a future in them. But gradually since then, the fundamentals have grown on me - I mean, look at the computer, then the software: the compiler, the database management system, the operating system - we humans have created some very amusing things. And I love being amused; it leads to fascination, and eventually, creation. Nowadays, I find myself amused by DS&A. This fresh approach with which I have begun solving programming problems is a part of this amusement. <br/><br/>
I want to keep feeling at least this much amusement forever.
