I have decided to revisit data structures and algorithms. But this time, with a different approach. I will surely write about it some other day. For now, I will focus on this question - why does this subject of computer science engineering exist anyway?  

Probably, it was never explicitly talked about. Or maybe, even if it was - neither of us, the teacher and the student, took it seriously. At the same time, I'd agree that one gets the answer gradually as they learn. This morning, I specifically tried to establish a consistent model about this in my mind; here is what I came up with:

<br/> 

_Computer Science Engineering is an applied subject. This means that the happiness of a computer science practitioner wouldn't primarily lie in reading about the subject but in being able to apply it to some cause in the real world. That is to say - fulfill some objective.  
Computer Science Engineering is about building computing systems that fulfill some objective and work towards some bigger objective. For instance, the internet serves the objective of quick and effective global collaboration and communication. Computer Science Engineering is about building the internet._

<br/> 

_These systems would need to deal with data. A lot of data. And with dealing, I mean two things - store the data and get answers from this stored data. To further ground 'getting answers' in technical terms - retrieving this data and running computations on this stored data. Data structures enable storing and retrieving data effectively. Algorithms let computations run efficiently. Therefore, it is essential to learn about these to build systems that can actually work in the real world. They form the very fundamentals of every system._

---

An addendum:   
I have decided to understand these bottom-up using C. There are various reasons for revisiting this language. To condense into just one sentence - 'It is just the right level of abstraction I need'. It has a digestible scope of available interfaces to deal with memory. Moreover, it doesn't have many high-level data structures like HashMap or LinkedLists already implemented. This means I will be implementing them myself using these limited memory interfaces I have. This setting is just perfect to teach me about things I have probably skimmed through earlier but are the building blocks of building systems.
