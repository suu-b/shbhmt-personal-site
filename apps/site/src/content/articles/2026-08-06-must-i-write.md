I like to call myself a poet, though I write only meagrely for publication. A few months ago, I was actively sending them out to journals and incessantly getting rejected. As expected, I grew doubtful. But not of my skills nor whether I could really be a poet, instead - of the very origin of my works. Whether they are truly mine or just a mosaic of all those poets' language and aesthetic choices whom I have read and consequently decided to become one.
I searched through the net and came across 'Letters to a Young Poet' - a book by Rainer Maria Rilke. I had heard this person's name before - maybe on YouTube. Or I suspect that I may have read his poems during my schooling. Either way, I decided to purchase it - it arrived, and I finished it in a few sittings.

---

The first letter broadly advised me (though it asked Mr Kappus, but since I was kinda impersonating him by reading the letters addressed to him, I would consider them written to me) to examine the very source from where my life springs. It asked to determine if I would die if writing was denied to me. Condensed into three words - 'Must I write'?  To determine this, retrospection is inevitable. I spent that night looking at my life as it is. This whole enterprise was sometimes sorta depressing and uplifting other times. However it went - I established this: <br/>
_Given a hyperconscious person of closed nature who feels a lot, gets overwhelmed but still strives to be a good person -  such a person has to have a way to express his inner self. Since my childhood, I expressed myself in different ways (crying counts). And most of these times, I have found myself writing. Perhaps that is how I ended up being a poet. This has helped to establish a connection with my inner self. Introspection opens the way for bigger things. Things that I have always wanted to do. Things of personal and societal meaning. Such is a life I have idealized._<br/>
Therefore, 'tis true that I have to write if I have to live a life I have idealized.
