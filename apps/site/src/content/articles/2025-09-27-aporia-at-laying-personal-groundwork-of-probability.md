Recently, I have picked up a habit of 'justifying' the very 'existence' of the new subjects/topics I pursue. This initiative consolidates my foundational understanding of the subject. It is really helpful in building and effectively wiring a mental model of the subject. And for me, a clearly wired mental model of a subject is a subject half-conquered.

For my research-related aims, recently, I decided to look at the concepts of probability (some again, and others for the first time). And I did the process mentioned in the previous paragraph. Thought it would be a good idea to jot them here.

---

Think we can start building the argument from this commonly agreed-upon human condition:  
<br />

_What to choose and what not, and later, bearing the consequences of choosing and not choosing is the ill-fated human condition_
<br />

It is conceived that choices are the predominant factor influencing our lives. This naturally follows that one who realizes this shall look to choose the most valuable option. This introduces Ethics, which dictates 'what value means to the looker, and by extension, what option is valuable?'.

In a utilitarian sense, the consequences of each option are more worthwhile to think about than the options themselves. At the same time, we realize that 'how things will be' is not linear in a reductionist sense, and instead is more effectively understood as a 'web' than a 'chain'. This uncertainty of 'what will happen' fills dread in humans.

Defn: Probability Framework is a mathematical framework that provides humans with the toolset to combat this uncertainty.
To combat uncertainty, it provides tools to reason about the chances of the desired (or undesired) consequence occurring.

The formula goes as follows:

$$
P(A) = \frac{\text{Number of favorable outcomes}}{\text{Total number of possible outcomes}},
\qquad 0 \leq P(A) \leq 1.
$$

Imagine tossing a coin: there are two possible outcomes - Heads or Tails.
Given an event A: Heads comes up.
As per the formula, the probability is 1/2. But what does it actually mean? Why such a fraction?
Well, it means that if you tossed a coin in two different worlds, you would get a head in one. So, it conveys: whenever you toss a coin, it is tossed in one more world where the opposite result comes up!

This metaphor serves the purpose well by incorporating famous parallel realities concepts in building the intuition. In this case, we can see that there is an equal number of favorable and unfavorable worlds. What if there aren't?
The metaphor fails there. Surely, there is something more to probability than merely counting worlds.

Thus, I questioned whether there is an all-agreed interpretation of probability, and I came to know there isn't. There are frequentists, Bayesians, and classical(s). There are a few other interpretations, too. I've decided to further look into them to understand, or maybe do some analysis on my own, to truly understand what they aim at.
